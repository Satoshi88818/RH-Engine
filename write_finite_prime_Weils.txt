The factorization of Section 17.7 failed for one ansatz. A different prime-indexed structure comes from Connes, Consani and Moscovici (arXiv:2511.22755; earlier Connes–Consani, arXiv:2106.01715): restrict the Weil quadratic form to test functions supported on $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, so that only the primes $p\le\lambda^2$ enter, and study its low-lying eigenvectors. They report that the eigenvalues are extremely small and that the spectra of associated operators approach the lowest zeros of $\zeta(\tfrac12+is)$ as the parameters grow; proving that convergence would prove RH (their statement). Our Hann packets are in the same class (support $|v|\le a$, $\lambda=e^a$, primes to $e^{2a}$). I implemented a version of the form myself (not their operators) and ran it at high precision.

**Setup.** Basis $\cos(k\pi v/a)$, $k=0,\dots,K$, on $|v|\le a$ (Gram matrix diagonal). The matrix $A=\text{Main}-\text{Prime}$ is built from primes only, at 60 digits (mpmath), with Gauss–Legendre quadrature for the archimedean term, and the generalized eigenproblem $A c=\varepsilon\,G c$ is solved. I then examined the Fourier transform $\hat\varphi_c(r)=\int\varphi_c(v)e^{irv}dv$ of the eigenvector $c$.

**$\zeta$, $a=1$, $K=19$: primes $2,3,5,7$ (and the power $4$) only.**

**What this shows**

- **The phenomenon reproduces, from primes alone.** Four primes at $a=1$ give the first eight zeta zeros to $11$–$24$ digits as zeros of an explicit function built from the lowest eigenvector. It is a prime-indexed structure of the kind the factorization hunt was after, and it works where the $\log n$-sampled Gram ansatz of Section 17.7 failed.
- **It is a consequence of the explicit formula, not of the Euler product or of RH.** The identical effect appears for the Davenport–Heilbronn function, which has no Euler product (and has zeros off the line, though at heights above this band). The mechanism is $A=V^\dagger V$ (Section 17.7): $A$ has as many tiny eigenvalues as the basis has directions beyond the zeros in its frequency band, and the eigenvector with the smallest $\sum_\gamma|\hat\varphi(\gamma)|^2$ must nearly vanish at the lowest zeros. Counting: at $a=1$ the band $r\lesssim K\pi/a\approx60$ holds about $13$ zeta zeros against $20$ basis functions and $6$–$7$ tiny eigenvalues; for DH it holds about $27$ zeros against $20$ functions and only one tiny eigenvalue, which is why $a=1$ fails for DH and $a=2$ succeeds.
- **So the numerical agreement does not distinguish $\zeta$ from a function with off-line zeros.** What would distinguish them is positivity of the whole form (no negative eigenvalue) at all heights, which is the open statement. Here every eigenvalue computed is positive, because no off-line zero of the DH function lies in the band tested.
- **Limits.** One basis, $a=1$ and $2$, $K=19$. I did not reproduce the operators $D_{\log}$ or the radical conditioning of Connes–Consani–Moscovici, nor test convergence as $\lambda,N\to\infty$ (the step they say would prove RH). The double-precision failure shows the regime is numerically delicate.
