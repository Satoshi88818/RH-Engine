# Pushing to γ ≈ 10¹¹, comparing with Odlyzko's tables, and testing t(x) ~ x^(−2/3) window by window

## Summary

1. **Survey to γ ≈ 10¹¹ (and 2.7×10¹¹) done.** About 1.95 million zeros at heights 10⁷–10¹¹ plus 2.0 million between 10³ and 1.13×10⁶. Counts match the Riemann–von Mangoldt count to within ±1.1 in every run.
2. **Odlyzko comparison done (through a GitHub mirror; see caveat).** All 2,000,403 table zeros in 1000 < γ < 1,132,490.66 match mine one-to-one. The 25 closest pairs are the same set and their gaps agree to 3.0e-09. At γ ≈ 2.68×10¹¹ all 10,000 table zeros match, and the 12 closest pairs are the same set (gaps agree to 5e-07).
3. **A survey bug was found through that comparison and fixed** (a close pair straddling a window boundary). All results below use the fixed survey. The numbers in `README_rs_scaling.md` came from the earlier version and should be treated as superseded by this file.
4. **Lemma at the top end:** for the 9 closest pairs at 10¹⁰, 10¹¹ and 2.7×10¹¹, the measured collision time is 1.0001–1.0010 times r²/8.
5. **Window-by-window test:** the smallest gap in a window of N₀ zeros follows the random-matrix extreme-value law, with ν_min ∝ N₀^(−1/3). Pooled over 58,467 (dataset, N₀) windows, the 5% and 1% coverage levels come out at 0.955 and 0.991 (expected 0.95 and 0.99). This supports a first-collision time that scales like N₀^(−2/3)/log²x, hence about x^(−2/3) for all zeros in [x, 2x] **if the same law keeps holding at far larger N₀ than I can test**.

This is evidence about how "barely true" RH would be. It is not a proof, and it says nothing about whether a zero is off the line.

## 1. Survey, and the bug

The survey is the one in `README_rs_scaling.md` (fast Riemann–Siegel Z, coarse grid 0.04, refinement at 0.002 for close pairs). It now scales to γ ≈ 10¹¹; the evaluator error is about 10⁻⁶ at 10¹¹ and 7×10⁻⁶ at 2.7×10¹¹ (measured against `mpmath`), which only matters for gaps below about 0.003.

**The bug.** In the first version, windows were contiguous. A close pair straddling a window boundary was split across two windows, so neither window saw both zeros and the refinement never triggered. Near 2.7×10¹¹ one such pair had gap 0.0241 against the table's 0.02243 (a 7% error), and the worst 10⁻⁴-size position errors in the zeros6 comparison all sat within 0.02 of a window boundary. **Fix:** windows are now 60 wide and overlap, and a window only accepts roots from its central 50. After the fix the same pair agrees with the table to 10⁻⁸.

## 4. Window-by-window test of the extreme-value law

**Claim tested.** For a window of N₀ consecutive zeros, ν_min (the smallest normalised gap) follows P(ν_min > x) ≈ exp(−N₀F(x)) with F(x) = (π²/9)x³ − (2π⁴/225)x⁵. Then u = N₀F(ν_min) should be Exp(1), the median of ν_min should scale like N₀^(−1/3), and the window's first-collision time τ = gap²/2 = (2π²/L²)ν_min² should scale like N₀^(−2/3)/L². I partitioned each dataset into non-overlapping windows of N₀ ∈ {100 … 30000} zeros.

| dataset | N₀ | windows | KS p (u vs Exp(1)) | mean u | coverage of 5% quantile (expect 0.95) | coverage of 1% quantile (expect 0.99) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pub. zeros6 [1e+03,1e+04) | 300 | 31 | 0.01 | 1.77 | 0.935 | 1.000 |
| pub. zeros6 [1e+04,1e+05) | 1,000 | 127 | 0.00 | 1.52 | 0.976 | 1.000 |
| pub. zeros6 [1e+05,1e+06) | 1,000 | 1609 | 0.00 | 1.15 | 0.958 | 0.989 |
| pub. zeros6 [1e6,1.13e6) | 1,000 | 253 | 0.28 | 1.14 | 0.960 | 0.996 |
| pub. zeros3 (~2.7e11) | 300 | 33 | 0.29 | 1.17 | 0.939 | 0.970 |
| mine 1e7 | 1,000 | 500 | 0.44 | 1.06 | 0.944 | 0.996 |
| mine 1e8 | 1,000 | 500 | 0.04 | 1.12 | 0.950 | 0.986 |
| mine 1e9 | 1,000 | 500 | 0.06 | 1.05 | 0.958 | 0.992 |

(N₀ = 1000 shown, or the largest available for small datasets; `window_test.json` has all N₀.)

- **Coverage:** pooled over all 58,467 windows, 0.955 of windows have first-collision time above the predicted 5% quantile (expected 0.95), and 0.991 above the 1% quantile (expected 0.99). It is slightly conservative, which matches the small-gap deficit.
- **Exponent:** the slope of log(median ν_min) against log N₀ is -0.30, -0.35, -0.36, -0.36, -0.34, -0.32, -0.33, -0.32 for the 8 datasets with at least 3 window sizes (theory −0.33; mean -0.335).
- **Where it fails:** KS p-values are tiny at small N₀ and low heights (the small-gap deficit; mean u is 1.1–1.5 below 10⁶) and become acceptable from 10⁷ up (p between 0.04 and 0.9 for N₀ ≥ 1000). Mean u is 1.0–1.3 at 10⁷ and above.

## 5. What this says about t(x)

For all zeros in [x, 2x] there are N ≈ x(ln(x/2π) + 2 ln 2 − 1)/2π of them, and the median first-collision time is, to leading order,

> τ_med(x) ≈ (2π²/L²)·(9 ln 2/(π²N))^(2/3) ∝ x^(−2/3) L^(−8/3), L = ln(x/2π).

The window test above supports the N₀^(−2/3) factor from N₀ = 100 to 30,000; the extrapolation to N ≈ 10¹¹–10²² is **not tested** and rests on the GUE small-gap law continuing to hold.

| x | N zeros in [x, 2x] | τ, 5% quantile | τ, median |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1e+09 | 3.1e+09 | 3.4e-09 | 1.9e-08 |
| 1e+11 | 3.8e+11 | 8.9e-11 | 5.0e-10 |
| 1e+13 | 4.5e+13 | 2.6e-12 | 1.4e-11 |
| 1e+15 | 5.3e+15 | 7.9e-14 | 4.5e-13 |
| 1e+18 | 6.4e+18 | 4.8e-16 | 2.7e-15 |
| 1e+20 | 7.1e+20 | 1.6e-17 | 9.3e-17 |
| 1e+22 | 7.8e+22 | 5.9e-19 | 3.3e-18 |

So a window criterion "no collision before t(x)" has to shrink like x^(−2/3) times a power of log x, not like 1/log²x. At x ≈ 10¹³ the median first-collision time is about 1.5×10⁻¹¹. By the two-body lemma the closest pair at that height would give Λ ≳ −1.5×10⁻¹¹ by the heuristic of `README_no_collision_bound.md`. That is the same order as the best published Λ lower bound (about −10⁻¹¹), which I am quoting from memory and have not re-verified. The published bound uses a more careful argument than my two-body heuristic, so this is an order-of-magnitude consistency check only.

## 6. Limitations

- Odlyzko's data came through a mirror (see §2).
- The window test covers N₀ up to 3×10⁴ and heights up to 2.7×10¹¹. The x^(−2/3) law for whole ranges [x, 2x] is an extrapolation of 7–11 orders of magnitude in N.
- At lower heights (below about 10⁶) the GUE small-gap law over-predicts close pairs, so the window test rejects it there. The law is approached from the conservative side.
- The lemma is exact for finite real systems; its extension to the infinite system is assumed.
- Nothing here says anything about a zero off the critical line.

## 7. Next steps

- Compare with the Odlyzko 10²¹/10²² tables. My evaluator cannot reach those heights without a different algorithm (FFT-based Odlyzko–Schönhage).
- Test the (WNC) idea directly: choose t(x) = τ₅%(x), flow each window back to t(x), and check that all zeros stay real, using the forward-flow tool for t > 0.
- Improve the small-gap model (finite-height corrections to GUE) so that the lower-height deficit is predicted, not just observed.
