## Summary

Li's criterion says RH is equivalent to λ_n ≥ 0 for every n ≥ 1, where λ_n = Σ_ρ [1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n] over the non-trivial zeros. It is the cleanest place where an arithmetic quantity (derivatives of log ξ at s = 1, i.e. Stieltjes-type constants) meets the zeros. I computed the λ_n both ways and asked how much a finite positivity check can tell us.

1. **The arithmetic side and the zero side agree to {dif:.0e} (relative) for every n ≤ 800.** The arithmetic side uses Taylor coefficients of log ξ at s = 1 (a Cauchy integral, 150-digit arithmetic); the zero side uses Odlyzko's 2,001,052 published zeros plus a smooth-density tail. ## 1. Method

**Arithmetic side.** Since Σ_ρ ln[(1 − (1 − 1/ρ)z)/(1 − z)] = ln ξ(1/(1 − z)) − ln ξ(1), we have λ_n/n = [zⁿ] ln ξ(1/(1 − z)). Writing ln ξ(1 + u) = ln ξ(1) + Σ a_k u^k gives λ_n = n Σ_{{k=1}}^{{n}} C(n−1, k−1) a_k. I obtained the a_k from a Cauchy integral of ln ξ on |u| = 13 (the nearest zero of ξ(1+u) is at distance 14.14), with 4096 points and 150 digits, unwrapping the phase continuously. A second run with a different radius, point count and precision (12.5, 3072, 160 digits) agrees with the first to every digit I compared (60 digits).

## 3. What this says about the missing input

- A violation of RH, if it exists, is invisible to any finite part of the Li sequence we could compute for heights where RH is already known. The criterion is an infinite family of positivity conditions; its content lies in "for all n", not in any computed value.
- As I recall, Bombieri and Lagarias showed that Li's criterion is a particular case of Weil's positivity criterion (the explicit-formula functional is non-negative on test functions g∗g̃). I did not recompute that and have not checked the statement. If it is right, the same conclusion applies to any finite family of test functions.
- In function fields the corresponding positivity comes from geometry (it is what makes Weil's theorem a theorem). For ζ no analogue is known, and nothing computed here substitutes for one.

## 4. Limitations

- For n > 800 the "true" λ_n in the detection analysis is the asymptotic formula (about 0.3% off at n = 800), and for n ≤ 800 the exact arithmetic value; n* depends on this only logarithmically.
- The detection analysis adds one quartet to the true zeros; it ignores any other hypothetical zeros, and the estimate formula is first order in δ/γ₀² (verified by exact scans up to γ₀ = 1000).
- I did not compute the Weil functional for other test functions.
- Nothing here bears on whether RH is true.

## 5. Possible next steps

- Compute the Weil functional for other families of test functions (for example Gaussians of varying width) and look at where positivity is tight.
- Compute the Nyman–Beurling/Báez-Duarte distances, a different arithmetic criterion, and see how their convergence rates compare.
