**A realistic Lean 4 certificate for this computation takes the following form.**

The numerical script verifies that the arithmetic Weil matrix \(M\) (for a real primitive character \(\chi\), support parameter \(L\), and basis size \(N\)) is close to the spectral side coming from the zeros and has positive eigenvalues. A formal certificate turns the positivity statement into a kernel-checked theorem.

### What can be certified today

For the concrete parameters \(L = 0.9\), \(N = 6\) and the two characters shown, the following is feasible with current Mathlib + a modest amount of certified arithmetic:

1. **Hard-code a rational (or dyadic) approximation \(M_0\) of the arithmetic matrix** together with a rigorous a-priori error bound \(\varepsilon\) such that
   \[
   \|M - M_0\|_\infty \le \varepsilon.
   \]
   (The script already shows that after the elementary tail the discrepancy is \(\sim 10^{-5}\); a careful interval evaluation of the integral + prime-power sum yields a concrete \(\varepsilon\).)

2. **Prove that \(M_0\) is positive definite** (e.g. all leading principal minors positive, or a certified Cholesky factorisation with residual control, or Gershgorin + strict diagonal dominance after a congruence, or the characteristic polynomial having no non-positive real roots via Sturm or interval methods).

3. **Transfer positivity** to the true matrix \(M\) by a perturbation argument:
   \[
   \lambda_{\min}(M) \ge \lambda_{\min}(M_0) - C_N\varepsilon > 0,
   \]
   where \(C_N\) is an explicit constant coming from the matrix norm (available in Mathlib).

4. **Link to the Weil criterion** for the completed \(L\)-function of \(\chi\): positivity of the quadratic form on the \(N\)-dimensional space of test functions spanned by the chosen basis implies that those particular test functions satisfy the Weil positivity condition. (The full RH for \(L(s,\chi)\) requires positivity for *all* admissible test functions; this is a finite-dimensional certificate that can later be fed into a larger inductive or density argument.)

### Practical next steps to make the certificate complete

- **Extract exact matrix entries.** Re-run the Python script (or a pure-mpmath version) writing the matrix to a JSON/CSV of rationals or high-precision floats; convert to Lean `ℚ` or `Float` with explicit error.
- **Certified assembly.** Replace the numerical quadrature and the sum over primes by interval arithmetic (or exact rational arithmetic for the finite prime-power sum up to \(e^{2L}\)). The archimedean integral can be bounded by a rigorous Gauss–Legendre quadrature with remainder or by a power-series expansion of the integrand.
- **Positivity engine.** For \(N=6\) the characteristic polynomial is degree 6; Sturm’s theorem or a Descartes-rule + interval isolation of real roots is entirely feasible and already has partial support in Mathlib / community libraries. Alternatively, compute the Cholesky factor over intervals and prove the residual is smaller than the smallest diagonal entry.
- **Integration with existing formalisations.** The Loeffler–Stoll formalisation of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions in Mathlib already supplies the analytic objects. The various “Weil positivity ⇒ RH” frameworks that have appeared (explicit-formula reductions, compact-support criteria, etc.) can take a finite-dimensional positivity certificate as a building block.
