1. **Archimedean-only positivity window**  
   For every even test function (or every vector in the discretized basis used by `WeilChar`) whose support lies in \((-L,L)\) with \(L<L_c(\zeta)\), the archimedean matrix \(M_{\mathrm{arch}}\) is positive-definite. 2. **Passage to the full Weil form**

   \[
   -\sum_{n<e^{2L}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(f(\log n)+f(-\log n)\bigr)
   \]
   while still controlling the lowest eigenvalue.  The offset
   \[
   L_c-\tfrac12\log 2\;\approx\;+0.025
   \]
   quantifies exactly how much “room” exists beyond the classical threshold before the first prime can force indefiniteness.

3. **Practical numerical / formal pipeline for \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\)**  
   - Fix a finite basis of even functions supported in \([-L,L]\) with \(L\) a little larger than \(L_c\) (or a Galerkin / Toeplitz truncation of the same size).  
   - Assemble the full matrix = archimedean Gram + prime-power Gram (only finitely many primes appear).  
   - Certify that the lowest eigenvalue remains positive (interval arithmetic, ball arithmetic, or exact rational arithmetic).  
   - The resulting positivity statement is precisely a finite fragment of Weil’s criterion for the zeta function of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\).
